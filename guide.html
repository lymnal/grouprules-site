How to automatically group tabs in Chrome

Updated September 30, 2026 · 4-minute read

Chrome's tab groups turn a wall of tabs into a few labelled, coloured bundles you can collapse. The catch is getting tabs into them. There are three ways, from fully manual to fully automatic.

On this page
  1. Group tabs by hand
  2. Let Chrome suggest groups (Tab organizer)
  3. Group tabs automatically with rules
  4. Which one should you use?
  5. Tips that make tab groups better

1. Group tabs by hand

  1. Right-click a tab and choose Add tab to new group.
  2. Type a name, pick a colour, and press Enter.
  3. To add more tabs, drag them into the group. Or select several at once: hold Ctrl (⌘ on Mac) and click each tab, then right-click and choose Add tabs to new group.

Good for: a handful of tabs. Not good for: keeping up as you browse. Every new tab has to be filed by hand, again.

2. Let Chrome suggest groups (Tab organizer)

Chrome has a built-in AI feature called Tab organizer. Right-click a tab and choose Organize similar tabs, and Chrome suggests groups with names, which you can accept or adjust.

  • It's a one-off tidy: it suggests groups when you ask it to, and doesn't keep sorting new tabs as you open them.
  • It groups by topic, as the AI understands it, so the same site can land in different groups on different days.
  • It needs you signed in to Chrome, and it isn't available in every country or on every work or school account.

Good for: a quick clean-up of a messy window. Google's own instructions are in Chrome Help.

3. Group tabs automatically with rules

  3. Press Alt+Shift+G to sort the window you have open now.

Tips that make tab groups better

  • Collapse a group by clicking its name. The tabs stay open, just out of the way.
  • Keep a group for later. Recent versions of Chrome save your groups, and they appear under the Tab groups button on the bookmarks bar or in the ⋮ menu. To see them on your other computers, turn on Saved tab groups in Chrome's sync settings.
  • Name groups with a word you'd search for. Chrome's tab search (Ctrl/⌘+Shift+A) finds tabs faster when groups have clear names.
  • Pin what you always keep open, like your inbox or music. Pinned tabs sit outside groups, and GroupRules never touches them.
